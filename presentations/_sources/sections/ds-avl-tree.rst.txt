Overview
========
Try our `AVL Rotation Lab </interactive/avl-rotation.html>`_ to step through insertions, removals, balance checks, and rotations.

+ Definition: An AVL (Adelson-Velsky and Landis) tree is a self-balancing
  binary search tree data structure. It is designed to maintain a balanced tree
  structure at all times, ensuring that the height difference (balance factor)
  between the left and right subtrees of any node is limited to no more than
  one.
+ Worst case guarantee for search, insert, and delete is :math:`\Theta(\log n)`

Characteristics
---------------
+ Balance factor = height(left subtree) - height(right subtree)
+ Balance factor of 0, -1, or 1 is considered balanced
+ Insertion and deletion operations may cause the tree to become unbalanced, in
  which cases the balance factor of one or more nodes will be -2 or +2.
+ Self-balancing: balance restored by rotations during insertion and deletion
+ Balancing operations are performed bottom-up, starting with the deepest
  unbalanced node.

Self-balancing in AVL Trees
===========================
+ Find and restore the imbalance from bottom up

  1. Check the balance factor of each node.
  2. If the balance factor is -2 or +2, perform rotations to restore balance.

+ 4 types of imbalances and corresponding restoration by rotations

Type of Imbalances
------------------
+ Balance factor of -2 or +2 indicates an imbalance
+ Go to the longest subtree from top to bottom starting from the unbalanced
  node
+ **Always refer to the lowest unbalanced node first.** The higher unbalanced
  nodes may be fixed automatically when the lower ones are fixed.
+ Naming convention for imbalances:

  * Left-Left (LL)
  * Right-Right (RR)
  * Left-Right (LR)
  * Right-Left (RL)

Rotations to restore balance (cont.)
------------------------------------
+ Double rotations are named from the bottom up. E.g., Left-Right rotation
  means first a left rotation on the left child, then a right rotation on the
  unbalanced node.

What to remember
================
+ What is the imbalance type?

+ LL: Right rotation
+ RR: Left rotation
+ LR: Left-right double rotation
+ RL: Right-left double rotation
