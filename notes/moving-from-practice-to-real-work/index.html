Moving from practice to real work

What a small contribution can show students about how a research group or an industry team works.

November 27, 2026

When I think about what students can get from research or an internship, I picture a small task that gives them a view of the bigger work. Say a student joins a faculty research group and helps summarize survey responses from a study that follows people over a semester. At a group meeting, someone asks whether the people who skipped the last survey were different from those who completed it. The student can compare their earlier answers, bring back what they found, and hear how it changes what the group is willing to claim.

I can picture something similar on an internship. A student might help fix a report that counts canceled orders as sales. Talking with the people who use the report could show why the count matters for their weekly planning. The student can trace where the count comes from, make the change, and see what a teammate checks before it goes live. A small fix gives them a chance to see how a request reaches the team and how the team decides it’s ready.

Research openings can be limited, and an internship can be hard to fit around classes or a job. I’d like more students to have a chance to see how a group works while taking on a piece of the work themselves. It’s easier to picture what working in a field might feel like once you’ve been part of one of its ordinary decisions.
