Overview
========
.. container:: flex-container

  .. container:: half

    + **Definition**:

      * A computational method inspired by natural evolution.
      * Used to find approximate solutions to optimization and search problems.
      * Merges biology and computer science to solve complex challenges.

  .. container:: half

    + **Historical Context**

      * **Origins in Computer Science**:

        - Traces back to the work of John Holland in the 1960s.
        - Introduced the schema theorem to describe the functioning of Genetic Algorithms.

      * **Biological Inspiration**:

        - Deeply rooted in Charles Darwin's theory of natural selection.
        - Evolution of species through the survival of the fittest individuals.
        - Provides a foundation for computational algorithms.


Metaheuristic Paradigm
----------------------
+ **Definition**:

  * Derived from Greek words "meta" (beyond) and "heuriskein" (to search or
    find).
  * Strategies designed to find or select heuristics for good-enough solutions
    to optimization problems.
  * Especially effective with incomplete information or limited computation
    capacity.

+ **Examples of Metaheuristic Algorithms**:

  * Simulated Annealing.
  * Tabu Search.
  * Ant Colony Optimization.
  * Particle Swarm Optimization.

Basics of Genetics and Evolution
--------------------------------
+ **DNA, Genes, and Chromosomes**:

  * DNA is the carrier of genetic instructions in biological systems.
  * Organized into chromosomes, which are collections of genes.
  * Each gene represents specific genetic information or functions.

+ **Core Principles**:

  * **Survival of the Fittest**: Differential survival and reproduction based
    on beneficial traits.
  * **Reproduction**: Generation of new solutions based on "fitness" or
    suitability.
  * **Mutation**: Introducing genetic diversity via changes in DNA.
  * **Crossover (Recombination)**: Combining genetic information from two
    parent genes.

Core Components
---------------
+ **Individual and encoding**

  * An individual is a possible solution to the problem.
  * Encoded as a string of genes, often binary, integer, real, or sometimes permutation.

+ **Population**:

  * Collection of individual potential solutions.
  * Each individual is a possible solution, mostly encoded (e.g., binary
    string).
  * Initial population usually generated randomly.

+ **Fitness Function**:

  * Measures how good each solution is with respect to the problem.
  * For example: Shorter path length in a traveling problem might be more
    "fit".

Core Components (cont.)
-----------------------
+ **Selection**

  * Selects individuals for reproduction based on fitness.
  * Individuals with higher fitness are more **likely** to be selected.
  * Randomness + heuristics = **stochastic** selection.

+ **Crossover (Recombination)**

  * Combines genetic information from two parent individuals.
  * Produces offspring with genetic information from both parents.
  * Often performed with a crossover rate (e.g., 0.8) to control the frequency
    of crossover.

+ **Mutation**

  * Introduces genetic diversity by randomly changing genes.
  * Random flip for binary encoding; random swap for permutation encoding; etc.
  * Mutation rate (e.g., 0.01) controls the frequency of mutation.

+ **Replacement**

  * Replaces individuals in the population with new offspring.
  * Total replacement or partial replacement.
  * Often performed with an elitism rate (e.g., 0.1) to preserve the best
    individuals.

Code Framework
==============
A basic framework for a Genetic Algorithm is as follows::

  1. Generate initial population of size N.
  2. Evaluate the fitness of each individual in the population.

  3. WHILE (stopping condition not met):

    a. Select two parents based on their fitness.
    b. Perform crossover on the selected parents to produce offsprings.
    c. Apply mutation on offsprings with a certain probability.
    d. Evaluate the fitness of the new offsprings.
    e. Replace individuals in the current population with the new offspring
        based on a replacement strategy.

  4. Return the best solution found.

GA Details
==========
+ Encoding, Gene

  * Binary Encoding: Each gene is a bit (0 or 1).
  * Integer Encoding: Each gene is an integer value.
  * Real-valued Encoding: Each gene is a real number.
  * Permutation Encoding: Each gene represents a position in a sequence.

+ Individual

  * Binary String: e.g., "1100101".
  * Integer Array: e.g., [3, 5, 2, 8].
  * Real-valued Vector: e.g., [0.5, 1.2, 3.4].
  * Permutation: e.g., [2, 3, 1, 4] for ordering problems.

GA Details (cont.)
------------------
+ Selection Methods

  * Roulette Wheel Selection: Probability proportional to fitness.
  * Tournament Selection: Randomly select a group and choose the best.
  * Rank-based Selection: Rank individuals and select based on rank.

+ Crossover Techniques

  * Binary, Integer, Real-valued: One-point, Two-point, Uniform crossover.
  * Permutation: Order crossover (OX), Partially matched crossover (PMX).

+ Mutation Techniques

  * Binary: Bit flip mutation.
  * Integer: Random reset mutation.
  * Real-valued: Gaussian mutation.
  * Permutation: Swap mutation, Inversion mutation.

TSP Example
===========
+ Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP)

  * A classic optimization problem.
  * Objective: Find the shortest possible route visiting each city exactly once
    and returning to the origin city.

+ NP-hard Problem for exact solutions.
+ GAs provide a practical approach to find good-enough solutions.

  * Encoding: Permutation of city indices.
  * Fitness Function: Minimize the total distance **or** Maximizing the inverse of the total distance of the tour.
  * Selection: Tournament selection.
  * Crossover: Order crossover (OX).
  * Mutation: Swap mutation.
  * Replacement: Total replacement (with elitism).
  * Stop criterion: Fixed number of generations or no improvement over several generations.

Limitations of Genetic Algorithms
=================================
+ **No Guarantee of Optimal Solution**:

  - GAs excel at finding near-optimal solutions.
  - They might not always reach the absolute best solution.

+ **Parameter Sensitivity**:

  - GA performance can greatly vary based on parameter settings.
  - Tuning parameters like mutation rate, crossover rate, and population size
    may require trial-and-error or domain-specific knowledge.

+ **Computational Overhead**:

  - Depending on problem complexity and GA settings, convergence might require
    multiple generations and evaluations.
  - Potential for high computational costs, especially for larger problems.

+ **Premature Convergence**:

  - GAs can converge too early to a suboptimal solution.
  - Caused by a lack of genetic diversity in the population.

.. note:: While GAs have these limitations, they are still a powerful tool. It's essential to be aware of these challenges and account for them in application and design.
