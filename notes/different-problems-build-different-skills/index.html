Different problems build different skills

What programming contests, data challenges, and open project briefs give students a chance to practice.

November 27, 2026

I’ve been thinking about why I’d point students in a programming course toward both the International Collegiate Programming Contest (ICPC) and Kaggle. I can picture a room scheduling problem on either one. A programming contest might give me a list of events and ask how many rooms they need. A Kaggle prediction challenge might give me past records and ask how busy those rooms will be next week. I’d be curious about a different part of each problem.

In the contest version, I like the moment when a small detail changes the answer. If one event ends exactly when another begins, can they share a room? Once the rules settle that, I can see whether my idea handles it. In the prediction version, I’d wonder whether a model that did well on ordinary weeks would hold up during exams. A single score could look fine even if the model was off during the week when study space was hardest to find. Sample ICPC problems · Kaggle’s competition guide

A hackathon or a case drawn from campus life could start with something looser. Say students mention that they can’t find a quiet spot to study. An empty room on the schedule might be locked, or it might be right next to a busy hallway. The part I’d want to think about first is what students would actually find helpful. I like that I can keep the setting almost the same and change the kind of thinking the problem brings out. That’s what makes me interested in sharing a few different options with students.
